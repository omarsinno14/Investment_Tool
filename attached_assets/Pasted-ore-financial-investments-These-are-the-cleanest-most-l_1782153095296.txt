ore financial investments

These are the cleanest, most legitimate categories:

Investment Type	What it means
Stocks	Buying ownership in public companies like Apple, Tesla, NVIDIA, banks, etc.
ETFs	Baskets of stocks/bonds traded like stocks. Example: S&P 500 ETFs, dividend ETFs, tech ETFs.
Mutual funds	Professionally managed pooled funds. Similar idea to ETFs, but usually less flexible.
Bonds	Lending money to governments or companies in exchange for interest.
Treasury bills / GICs / term deposits	Lower-risk fixed-income products. Good for cash parking.
Dividend stocks	Companies that pay regular cash distributions.
Index funds	Passive funds that track markets like the S&P 500, Nasdaq, TSX, etc.
Money market funds	Short-term, lower-risk funds used for cash-like returns.

Regulators like the SEC list stocks, bonds, ETFs, mutual funds, money market funds, Treasury securities, annuities, and alternatives as common investment products.

Alternative investments

These feel more “opportunity marketplace” and could fit your app well.

Investment Type	What it means
Private equity	Investing in private companies before they go public or get acquired.
Venture capital	Investing in startups early. High risk, high upside.
Angel investing	Personally investing in startups, usually at early stages.
Private credit	Lending money to companies outside traditional banks.
Hedge funds	Actively managed funds using complex strategies. Usually for wealthy/accredited investors.
Private placements	Buying securities not offered publicly. Usually restricted and higher risk.
Business acquisition deals	Buying part or all of a small business.
Revenue-share deals	Investing in a company in exchange for a percentage of future revenue.
Litigation finance	Funding lawsuits in exchange for a share of potential settlement proceeds.
Music royalties	Buying rights to earn royalties from songs/catalogs.
Film/media financing	Funding movies, shows, or content projects.
Franchise investments	Investing in franchise locations or operators.
Infrastructure funds	Roads, ports, energy grids, telecom towers, etc.
Farmland / agriculture funds	Exposure to agricultural production without buying land directly.
Timber funds	Investing in forests/timber production.
Carbon credits	Exposure to emissions credits or environmental markets.

FINRA groups many products beyond stocks and bonds under “alternative and emerging products” and warns that risks vary heavily by product, so disclosure documents and suitability matter a lot.

Luxury / collectible investments

These are more speculative, but they match the “bourgeois minimal” luxury vibe.

Investment Type	Notes
Fine art	Paintings, sculptures, blue-chip artists, fractional art platforms.
Classic cars	Ferrari, Porsche, Mercedes, rare BMW M cars, etc.
Rare whisky	Limited bottles, casks, collectible distilleries.
Fine wine	Bordeaux, Burgundy, Champagne, investment-grade bottles.
Jewelry	High-end signed pieces, diamonds, rare gemstones.
Designer handbags	Hermès Birkin/Kelly, Chanel classics, limited releases.
Sneakers	Rare Jordans, Nike collabs, limited drops.
Sports cards	Graded rookie cards, rare historic cards.
Pokémon / trading cards	Sealed boxes, graded rare cards.
Rare books/manuscripts	First editions, signed copies, historic documents.
Coins	Gold coins, rare numismatic coins, sovereign coins.
Stamps	Very niche, but still a collectible market.
Vintage furniture/design	Mid-century, Italian design, collectible chairs/lighting.
Luxury pens	Montblanc limited editions, rare writing instruments.

Important: collectibles can look rich, but they are often illiquid, hard to price, expensive to insure/store, and full of fake/scam risk. They’re not as clean as stocks or ETFs.

Digital / modern investments
Investment Type	Notes
Bitcoin	The main crypto asset. High volatility.
Ethereum	Crypto network asset, also high volatility.
Crypto ETFs	Exposure to crypto through traditional brokerage accounts.
Stablecoin yield	Risky; depends heavily on platform, custody, and regulation.
Tokenized assets	Real-world assets represented on-chain. Emerging and risky.
Domain names	Buying valuable domains and reselling later.
Digital businesses	Websites, apps, newsletters, SaaS tools.
YouTube channels / content assets	Buying monetized digital media properties.
Apps / micro-SaaS	Buying small software businesses.

Crypto assets can be extremely volatile and investor education bodies warn that people can lose some or all of their money.

Business-style investments

These are great for an “opportunities” platform.

Investment Type	What it means
Small business equity	Buying a stake in a local/private business.
Silent partnership	You provide capital, someone else operates.
Franchise location funding	You help finance a new location.
Equipment financing	Funding machines, trucks, medical equipment, restaurant equipment, etc.
Inventory financing	Funding inventory for a business in exchange for return.
Merchant cash advances	Risky lending to small businesses based on future sales.
Invoice factoring	Buying unpaid invoices at a discount.
E-commerce brand acquisition	Buying Shopify/Amazon brands.
Car rental fleet investment	Funding vehicles for rental businesses.
Vending machines / ATMs	Cash-flowing physical assets.
Laundromats / car washes	Operating-business investments, not passive unless structured well.

For your app, this category is honestly very interesting because it feels like “deals”, not just boring investing.

Commodities and hard assets
Investment Type	Notes
Gold	Classic store-of-value asset.
Silver	More industrial and volatile than gold.
Platinum / palladium	Precious metals with industrial demand.
Oil / gas exposure	Through stocks, ETFs, futures, or private deals.
Uranium	Energy/nuclear exposure.
Copper	Infrastructure/electrification theme.
Agricultural commodities	Wheat, corn, soybeans, coffee, cocoa, etc.
Energy royalties	Income from oil/gas/mineral rights.

These can be invested in directly, through funds, through commodity-linked products, or through related companies.
